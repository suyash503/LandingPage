Step one

It starts on the counter.

Photograph the receipt before it goes in the bin. That is the entire ritual, and it is the only part a person has to remember.

Step two

Tallwood reads it.

Total, date, line items, category. You don't type anything — and you can correct anything, because scanning a crumpled receipt is not magic and we won't pretend it is.

Step three

Split it the way it actually happened.

Four ways, three ways, or Dan pays for his own oat milk. Tap a face to leave someone out and every share recalculates while you watch.

Step four

Then it does the part nobody likes.

A reminder goes out on the date the house agreed on, from the app rather than from you. Nobody has to be the one who asks, which is the whole reason the spreadsheet failed.

Step five

Once a month, it's zero.

One payment each, in both directions at once, and the house is square again. Then March closes and April starts counting.

Both sides

It only works if it works for everyone.

Every split lands on the other phone the moment you save it, with the receipt attached — so nobody has to take your word for the number.

Half of this product is arithmetic. The other half is not having the conversation.

The delay between the two screens is deliberate. Sync that looks instant reads as a mock-up; a beat of lag reads as software.

What it does

Four things, done properly.

Receipt scanning

Photograph it. Tallwood pulls the total, the date and the line items, and shows you what it read before it saves anything.

Uneven splits

By share, by percentage, or by item — for when only two of you drink the coffee that costs $11.50 a bag.

Recurring bills

Rent, internet, the boiler service. Set the amount and the day once, and it gets counted every month without anyone posting about it.

One-tap settle

One payment clears everything you owe to everyone, and everything owed to you, in the same movement.

What it doesn't do

The list most apps leave out.

Money apps have a habit of becoming something else once they have your data. Here is what this one has decided not to become.

  • NoAds.Not in the feed, not in the reminders, not in the monthly summary email.
  • NoSelling your transaction history.Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to a partner bank. There is no version of this business that involves it.
  • NoStreaks, badges or 9pm guilt.Reminders go to the person who owes money, on the date the house agreed. Nothing else notifies you, ever.
  • NoCredit scores or a card to upsell you.Tallwood is not a stepping stone to a lending product. It counts what four people spent on washing-up liquid.
  • NoMandatory bank login.Connect an account if you want the settle-up shortcut. Or don't, and run the whole house on receipts.

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

Does everyone in the house need the app?

For the reminders and the settle-up, yes. But you can add a housemate as a name only and still track what they owe — they'll get a plain text message with a link instead of a push notification. One holdout doesn't break the count.

What if someone just won't pay?

Tallwood can't make anyone pay, and it doesn't pretend to. What it does is remove the ambiguity — the amount, the receipt and the date are on record, so the conversation is about the money rather than about whose memory is right.

Do you touch my bank account?

Only if you connect one, and only to move the settle-up payment you approve. Nothing is read, categorised or stored beyond that transaction. The whole app works on receipts alone if you'd rather not.

What happens to my data when I move out?

You keep a full export of everything you were part of, and your personal record is deleted from the house within 30 days. The house keeps its own totals, with your name replaced by "former housemate".

Does it work across currencies?

Yes — each receipt keeps the currency it was paid in, and the house settles in whichever one it picked. Rates are taken on the settle date, and the rate used is shown on every line so nobody has to trust it blindly.

How is this different from a group chat and a spreadsheet?

The spreadsheet works. It just needs one person to maintain it forever, and that person eventually stops. Tallwood is the same arithmetic with nobody assigned to do it, plus the receipts attached and the reminders coming from software instead of a housemate.
