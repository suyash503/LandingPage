The quarterly slide

You already have the answer. It is in nine thousand messages nobody has time to read.

Every quarter someone on your team blocks out a week. They export tickets, skim call notes, paste the ugliest quotes into a deck, and present a roadmap. By the time it ships, the export is four months old.

The messages that never make the deck are the expensive ones. A churned account that told support three times. A theme that only shows up in sales calls, never in tickets. A complaint that seven of your ten largest customers made in different words.

You do not have a data problem. You have a reading problem — and it does not scale by hiring another PM.

How it works

Three steps. The first one takes ten minutes and the other two are not your job.

STEP 01

Connect what you already use

Zendesk, Intercom, Gong, Slack, app-store reviews, a CSV of churn surveys. Read-only tokens, scoped to the channels you pick. Nothing is written back without your say-so.

STEP 02

Throughline finds the themes

Messages are grouped by what they are actually about — not by tag, not by keyword. Each group is then weighted by the accounts behind it, so eleven mentions from your biggest customers outrank ninety from trials.

STEP 03

You ship the right thing

Open the top theme, read the twelve quotes and the accounts behind it, then push it to Jira or Linear with the evidence attached. When it ships, the theme’s trend line tells you whether it worked.

What you get

Six things, done properly.

Multi-source ingest

Tickets, call transcripts, reviews, NPS comments, churn surveys and a Slack channel, in one place. Backfill two years on the first sync.

Theme clustering

Grouped by meaning, not by tag. “Export times out” and “CSV never arrives” land in the same theme without anyone writing a rule.

Revenue weighting

Each theme carries the ARR of the accounts behind it, pulled from your CRM. Loud does not automatically beat valuable.

Trend over time

Watch a theme after you ship against it. If mentions do not fall, you fixed the wrong half of the problem — and you find out in weeks.

Jira and Linear push

Send a theme across as an issue with the quotes, the account list and the revenue figure attached. Two-way status sync, so it closes itself.

Audit trail

Every theme keeps the exact messages that formed it, who read it, what was pushed and when. Nothing is a black box you have to take on trust.

Questions

The things a product lead asks first.

How is this different from a feedback board?

A feedback board collects what customers bother to write down on a board. That is a self-selected few percent of the people who have an opinion, and it skews to whoever is loudest. Throughline reads the conversations that already happened — tickets they opened because something broke, calls your reps recorded, the reason someone gave when they cancelled. Nobody has to be asked to participate, so you see the themes people complain about but never formally request.

What happens to personal data in our tickets?

Names, emails, phone numbers and card fragments are detected and masked before a message is stored or clustered. You can add your own patterns — internal IDs, account numbers — and you can exclude whole channels. Masking runs before storage, so redacted values never sit in the index. On Enterprise you choose EU or US residency and set your own retention window.

How long does setup actually take?

Around ten minutes to authorise your first two sources, then the backfill runs on its own. A two-year history of a few hundred thousand messages usually finishes overnight, and you get your first ranked themes the next morning. Revenue weighting needs a CRM connection, which is another five minutes.

What if our volume is low?

Below roughly 400 conversations a quarter, clustering stops earning its keep — you can read 400 messages yourself, and you probably should. Throughline will still group and rank them, but be honest about whether you need the tool yet. Tell us your volume and we will say if it is too early.

Do you train models on our data?

No. Your conversations are used to produce your themes and nothing else. They are not used to train shared models, they are not pooled with other customers, and they are not used to build benchmarks. Deleting a source deletes its messages and the clusters derived from them.

How is “revenue at risk” calculated?

It is the sum of the annual contract value of every account that raised the theme at least once in the window — not a prediction of what you will lose. Each account is counted once no matter how often it complained, so one very vocal customer cannot inflate a theme. You can see the exact account list behind any figure, and you can switch the weighting to expansion pipeline or renewal date instead.
