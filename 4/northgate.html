The assembly

What you're paying for, from the deck up.

Every roof on this page is five layers and a great many nails. This is the order they go on in — and the parts a cheap quote quietly leaves out.

  1. 1
    Ice & water barrier

    A rubberised membrane running six feet up from the eave — the strip where ice dams form. It self-seals around every nail driven through it.

  2. 2
    Architectural shingles

    Laid in courses, each one nailed through the course beneath it. Six nails per shingle, not four. The difference shows up in the first real wind.

  3. 3
    Synthetic underlayment

    The second skin, over the whole deck. Not felt — felt tears under a roofer's boot on a July afternoon and you never see where.

  4. 4
    7/16" OSB deck

    Replaced by the sheet where it's soft, not by the roof. You get the sheet count in writing before anyone starts pulling shingles.

  5. 5
    Rafters and ventilation

    If the attic can't breathe, the deck cooks and the shingles age at double speed. We check the intake before we quote, not after.

  6. 6
    Drip edge and soffit intake

    Metal at the eave carries water off the fascia; the soffit lets air in beneath it. Both get skipped constantly, and both are cheap now and expensive later.

Before you call

The questions we get every week.

How close is the estimator to the real quote?

Within about 12% on a straightforward replacement. It knows your material, footprint, storeys and timing — it doesn't know about a rotted deck, three layers of old shingle, or a chimney that needs re-flashing. Those are what move the number, and they're what the inspection is for.

Do you do repairs, or only full replacements?

Both. If you've lost a dozen shingles off one slope we'll say so rather than sell you a roof — a repair on a roof with ten good years left is the right answer and we'd rather be the ones who told you.

What happens if my insurance denies the claim?

We read the adjuster's report, re-inspect, and file a supplement if there's a case. Roughly a third of the denials we re-open get reversed. If there's no case we'll tell you that too, and you'll have a written quote you can pay for directly.

How long does the work actually take?

A typical asphalt replacement is one to two days on site. Metal runs three to five. The wait to get on the calendar is the real variable — eleven days on average, longer in the four weeks after a hailstorm.

Are you licensed, and who's on my roof?

Licensed and insured in Minnesota, and our own crews do the work — no day-labour subcontracting. The foreman's name is on your quote before anyone shows up.

What does the 25-year warranty actually cover?

Our workmanship — flashing, fastening, ventilation, anything we installed wrong. Materials carry the manufacturer's own warranty on top, usually 30 to 50 years. It transfers once if you sell the house.
